Disney Adventure

Day 2: Animal Kingdom

Monday morning moved the family from the quiet of our villa at Give Kids the World Village, back into the rhythm of the wild — particularly Animal Kingdom. Randy, my son-in-law and lifetime Kurt Warner fan, paid special attention when he heard Kurt announce that if we're late the Dad of the guilty party had to sing in front of the whole group. Since Randy had zero interest in leading any form of serenade, we were not planning to be the ones who disappointed a Super Bowl champ.

Although Give the World has its own dining area, breakfast wasn't served early enough for us to grab it and be on time for our 8:30 am schedule. Therefore, we left plenty early in order to drive through for breakfast on the way. Finley, my granddaughter, and I were sitting in the backseat together, so I carefully placed my large Diet Coke in the drink holder while I made sure what we ordered got sorted out. Finley happily snacking, Avery looking satisfied, and me with my breakfast poised for consumption, I let Randy know that we were good to go. Whereupon he proceeded to move to the right while my large Diet Coke decided to make a left turn directly into my lap. Did I mention that we were on a trip with the Warner Family and being late was not an option?

[photo — Animal Kingdom gate, the group arriving]

After a hasty restroom clean-up which cut us close to our arrival time, we met at the gate, nametags in place, and — most of us — with dry pants. As we arrived, volunteers began to circle and greet everyone. While Sierra gathered our stroller and helped our two get settled, kids got reacquainted from the previous night's fun, and we headed as a herd of around forty into Animal Kingdom with our VIP Guides shepherding us towards a Safari and then to an indoor musical celebration of the Lion King.

The day's rhythm followed the itinerary's predicted path options — which would become more familiarly known to the group as "Thrill Seekers" and "Chill Seekers." Each day at this point in the schedule, Kurt would take those interested in faster rides while Brenda and Zack would accompany those who preferred less velocity in their Disney. On this day, Sierra and I stayed with the grandkids while their parents followed Kurt off to the roller coasters. Between the two of us, we continued through our day at Disney, riding rides, seeing shows, pushing strollers, and staying hydrated until we arrived together for lunch at the Rainforest Cafe where our family was reunited with the day's daredevils.

[photo — Rainforest Cafe lunch, the full group]

Once we were seated, drinks showed up, food appeared, platters of food filled the table, family-style; for those with special diets, beautiful, custom meals materialized. What felt like a Rainforest Cafe Banquet was set for this party of forty to fifty people who all came in — essentially at once — after a long morning of adventure in the Florida heat in June. Without a hitch, servers met each guest's needs in ways that make one underestimate the skill and planning for this frictionless facade. At this meal, our group didn't interact too much with the overall party; the tables were tight and we were all hungry after our morning adventures. Our volunteer, who sat at the long table with us and the other families as they trickled in, chatted with the kids and we all got caught up on each other's day as we passed plates up and down the long table.

As the meal ended, Kurt gathered everyone's attention to sum up the day and remind us of Tuesday's plan for the Magic Kingdom. Before he finished, he announced, much to his wife's chagrin — and delight, that it was "Ms. Brenda's birthday week," so all the people on our side of the Rainforest Cafe joyfully sang "Happy Birthday" to Ms. Brenda. After the cheering subsided, we all departed for our separate afternoons with many heading back to relax with unlimited slushies and ice cream by the pool at Give Kids the World Village.
